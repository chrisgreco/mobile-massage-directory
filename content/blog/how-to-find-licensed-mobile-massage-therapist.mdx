# How to Find a Licensed Mobile Massage Therapist Near You (7 Things to Check)

Inviting a massage therapist into your home requires a higher level of trust than walking into a spa. You need to know the person showing up at your door is qualified, insured, and professional. Here are seven things to verify before you book.

---

## 1. Verify Their Massage License

This is non-negotiable. Every US state except Vermont, Wyoming, Kansas, and Minnesota requires massage therapists to hold a state-issued license or certification. A licensed massage therapist (LMT) has completed a minimum number of education hours (typically 500 to 1,000 depending on the state), passed a national certification exam, and met their state's specific requirements.

**How to check:** Most states have an online license verification portal. Search for "[your state] massage therapy license lookup" and enter the therapist's name or license number. If a therapist cannot or will not provide their license number, that is an immediate red flag.

Some states use different titles:
- **LMT** — Licensed Massage Therapist (most common)
- **CMT** — Certified Massage Therapist
- **RMT** — Registered Massage Therapist
- **LMP** — Licensed Massage Practitioner (Washington state)

Nationally recognized credentials include **NCBTMB** (National Certification Board for Therapeutic Massage and Bodywork) certification, which requires passing a rigorous exam and maintaining continuing education.

---

## 2. Confirm They Carry Liability Insurance

A professional mobile massage therapist carries their own liability insurance policy, typically $1 million to $2 million in coverage. This protects both of you. If something goes wrong during a session — an allergic reaction to massage oil, a slip on a wet floor, or an injury claim — their insurance covers it.

**How to check:** Ask directly. A professional therapist will not hesitate to confirm they are insured. Some will provide a certificate of insurance on request. Membership in professional organizations like AMTA (American Massage Therapy Association) or ABMP (Associated Bodywork and Massage Professionals) includes liability insurance as a member benefit, so membership in either organization is a good sign.

---

## 3. Read Reviews From Real Clients

Online reviews are your best window into what a therapist is actually like to work with. Look for patterns across multiple reviews rather than fixating on a single comment.

**What to look for:**
- Consistent mentions of professionalism and punctuality
- Comments about communication before and during the session
- Notes about the therapist's setup process and cleanliness
- Whether clients rebooked (repeat clients are the strongest endorsement)

**Where to look:**
- Google Business Profile reviews
- Yelp
- Their profile on directory sites
- Social media testimonials

**Red flags in reviews:**
- Multiple complaints about showing up late
- Comments about unprofessional behavior or boundary issues
- Lack of any reviews at all (not necessarily bad if the therapist is new, but worth noting)

Aim for therapists with at least 10 reviews and an average rating of 4.5 or higher. A handful of negative reviews among many positive ones is normal — no one has a perfect record. But a pattern of similar complaints is a warning sign.

## 4. Check Their Modality Training and Specializations

Not all massage therapists are trained in every modality. A therapist who is excellent at Swedish relaxation massage may not have the advanced training needed for deep tissue work, prenatal massage, or lymphatic drainage.

**Questions to ask:**
- What modalities are you trained and certified in?
- How many hours of training do you have in [specific modality]?
- Do you have any advanced certifications or specializations?
- How long have you been practicing this particular technique?

This matters most for specialized needs. Prenatal massage requires specific training in safe positioning and contraindications during pregnancy. Lymphatic drainage is a precise technique that requires dedicated coursework. Sports massage for competitive athletes is different from a general relaxation massage.

If you have a specific condition — chronic back pain, post-surgical recovery, fibromyalgia — ask the therapist whether they have experience working with clients in similar situations.

## 5. Review Their Cancellation and No-Show Policy

Mobile therapists invest significant time and money traveling to each appointment. A fair cancellation policy protects their livelihood while giving you reasonable flexibility.

**Standard policies:**
- **24-hour cancellation** — No charge if you cancel at least 24 hours before your appointment
- **Late cancellation (under 24 hours)** — 50% of session cost
- **No-show** — Full session cost

Some therapists are more flexible, especially for first-time clients or emergencies. The key is knowing the policy upfront. A therapist who does not have a cancellation policy may not be running their practice professionally.

**What to clarify:**
- What counts as a "cancellation" vs. a "reschedule"?
- Is there a fee for rescheduling within 24 hours?
- What happens if the therapist needs to cancel — do you get priority rebooking?

## 6. Ask About Their Equipment and Setup

A professional mobile therapist brings everything needed for a high-quality session. You should not need to provide anything except a suitable space.

**Standard equipment a mobile therapist should bring:**
- Professional-grade portable massage table (padded, adjustable, with face cradle)
- Clean linens for each client (fitted sheet, flat sheet, face cradle cover, blanket)
- Professional massage oil, lotion, or cream
- Hand sanitizer and cleaning supplies
- Music (portable speaker or phone)
- Bolsters and pillows for positioning

**For specialty sessions:**
- Hot stone massage: Heated stones and a warming unit
- Aromatherapy: Essential oils and a diffuser
- Prenatal massage: Extra bolsters and a side-lying support system

**Space requirements:** You will need a room with at least 8 feet by 10 feet of clear floor space, a ceiling tall enough for the therapist to stand comfortably, and a comfortable room temperature (around 72-75 degrees Fahrenheit). A living room, bedroom, or home office usually works well. Carpet or a flat, non-slip surface is ideal.

---

## 7. Evaluate Their Communication and Professionalism

The way a therapist communicates before your first appointment tells you a lot about how they run their practice.

**Green flags:**
- They respond to inquiries within a few hours (or by the next business day)
- They ask about your health history, goals for the session, and any injuries or conditions
- They confirm the appointment details (address, time, session length, modality, price) clearly
- They provide their full name and license number without being asked
- They arrive on time and dressed professionally
- They do a brief intake conversation before starting

**Red flags:**
- Vague or evasive answers about licensing or insurance
- Pushy upselling before you have even had your first session
- Unwillingness to discuss their training or experience
- Poor grammar and professionalism in written communication (this may seem minor, but it often reflects how they run their practice overall)
- They ask to be paid in cash only with no receipt

---

## Putting It All Together

Before you book a mobile massage, run through this checklist:

- [ ] License verified through your state's online portal
- [ ] Liability insurance confirmed (or membership in AMTA/ABMP)
- [ ] At least 10 positive reviews with a 4.5+ average rating
- [ ] Trained and experienced in the specific modality you need
- [ ] Clear cancellation policy communicated upfront
- [ ] Brings all professional equipment (you just provide the space)
- [ ] Communicates promptly and professionally

A therapist who checks all seven boxes is someone you can trust in your home. Once you find a great mobile massage therapist, hold on to them — a long-term relationship with a therapist who knows your body and preferences is one of the best investments you can make in your health and well-being.
